AJMAN NUVENTURES CENTRE

FREE ZONE (ANCFZ)

Rules & Regulations

Licensing, Company Regulations & Amiri Decree

ANCFZ was established by Amiri Decree 15/2023, which sets out the free zone’s licensing and company regulations. Companies registered in the free zone must also follow applicable UAE federal requirements relating to beneficial ownership, anti-money laundering and corporate tax.

The documents listed below are the official texts referenced on the ANCFZ page. They cover the formation decree, violations and fines, beneficial ownership procedures and penalties, anti-money laundering regulations, and the corporate tax guide for free zone persons.


1. ANCFZ Regulatory Documents


ANCFZ Formation Amiri Decree 15/2023

المرسوم الأميري رقم (15) لسنة 2023 بشأن إنشاء مركز عجمان للمشاريع الجديدة

صدر المرسوم الأميري رقم (15) لسنة 2023 بشأن إنشاء مركز عجمان للمشاريع الجديدة في إمارة عجمان، بهدف دعم التنمية الاقتصادية وتنويع الأنشطة الاقتصادية وجذب الاستثمارات وتعزيز بيئة الأعمال في الإمارة.

أبرز الأحكام التنظيمية للمرسوم:

إنشاء المركز وصفته القانونية: يُنشأ المركز في إمارة عجمان بوصفه منطقة حرة تتمتع بالشخصية الاعتبارية والاستقلال المالي والإداري، وتكون له الأهلية القانونية اللازمة لتحقيق أهدافه وممارسة اختصاصاته.

أهداف المركز: تشجيع الاستثمار والمشاريع الاقتصادية والإبداعية والمبتكرة، وتعزيز القدرة التنافسية للإمارة، وتوفير بيئة ملائمة لممارسة الأنشطة الاقتصادية، ودعم الصناعات القائمة على الطاقة البديلة والمتجددة، مع المحافظة على الموارد الطبيعية وتحسين الصحة العامة والبيئة.

اختصاصات المركز: تنظيم وترخيص الأنشطة والأعمال المسموح بمزاولتها داخل المركز، ووضع القواعد والشروط والإجراءات المنظمة لها، والإشراف والرقابة على المنشآت المرخصة، وتوفير المرافق والخدمات اللازمة، وإدارة الأصول والأموال، وإبرام الاتفاقيات والشراكات ذات الصلة باختصاصاته.

الإدارة والحوكمة: يتولى رئيس المركز اعتماد السياسات والخطط الاستراتيجية واللوائح والأنظمة والميزانية، والإشراف على أعمال المركز. ويتولى المدير التنفيذي إدارة العمليات اليومية، وتنفيذ السياسات والقرارات، وإعداد اللوائح والموازنات والتقارير، والإشراف على الموظفين والشؤون المالية والإدارية.

الأنشطة والبضائع: ينظم المرسوم ممارسة الأنشطة الاقتصادية والتجارية داخل المركز، واستيراد البضائع وتخزينها وتداولها وتصديرها، وفقًا للأحكام والضوابط المقررة. كما يحدد أنواع البضائع المحظور دخولها إلى المركز، بما يشمل البضائع الفاسدة أو التالفة، والمواد المخدرة، والأسلحة والذخائر غير المرخصة، والبضائع المخالفة للقوانين أو الآداب العامة أو حقوق الملكية الفكرية.

الموارد والشؤون المالية: تتكون الموارد المالية للمركز من الأموال المخصصة له من الحكومة، وعوائد استثماراته، وإيرادات الإيجارات والرسوم والغرامات، وأي موارد مالية أخرى يوافق عليها رئيس المركز. وتُنظم حساباته وميزانيته وفقًا للأحكام المالية المعتمدة.

فض المنازعات والمسؤولية: ينص المرسوم على تشكيل لجنة لفض المنازعات، وينظم المسائل المتعلقة بالمسؤولية والضبطية القضائية وإصدار اللوائح التنفيذية اللازمة لتطبيق أحكامه.

النفاذ والنشر: يعمل بالمرسوم اعتبارًا من 1 يناير 2024، ويُنشر في الجريدة الرسمية للإمارة.

ANCFZ Violations and Fines

لائحة المخالفات والغرامات المفروضة على المنشآت المرخصة لدى مركز عجمان للمشاريع الجديدة (ANCFZ)

تحدد اللائحة المخالفات والغرامات المقررة على المنشآت المرخصة لدى مركز عجمان للمشاريع الجديدة (المنطقة الحرة)، بهدف ضمان الالتزام بالأنظمة والتعليمات المعمول بها وتنظيم الأنشطة داخل المنطقة الحرة.

أبرز فئات المخالفات:

مخالفات شؤون العمل: تشمل عدم استكمال تصاريح الإقامة أو العمل ضمن المدد المحددة، وتشغيل العمالة المخالفة أو القُصّر، ومخالفة متطلبات تصاريح العمل، وعدم الالتزام بحقوق الموظفين ومتطلبات الرواتب والإجازات.

مخالفات المكاتب: تشمل تخزين البضائع داخل المكاتب، والنوم أو التدخين فيها، وتشغيل الورش، وتخزين المواد الخطرة، والإخلال بالنظافة.

المخالفات الصحية والبيئية: تشمل التخلص غير السليم من النفايات ومياه الصرف الصحي والمواد الكيميائية، وتجاوز مستويات الانبعاثات أو الضوضاء المسموح بها، وعدم الالتزام بمتطلبات التهوية والمعايير البيئية.

مخالفات السلامة المهنية والأمن: تشمل عدم توفير معدات الوقاية وأنظمة مكافحة الحريق، وعدم استيفاء متطلبات السلامة والدفاع المدني، وإعاقة مخارج الطوارئ أو الوصول إلى معدات السلامة، والتخزين غير الآمن للمواد والبضائع.

مخالفات النظافة وإدارة النفايات: تشمل إهمال نظافة المنشآت ومحيطها، والتخلص العشوائي من النفايات، وحرق المخلفات، والتعامل غير السليم مع النفايات الخطرة.

مخالفات التراخيص وحماية المستهلك: تشمل ممارسة الأنشطة الاقتصادية دون ترخيص أو خارج نطاق الأنشطة المعتمدة، وعدم تجديد التراخيص في المواعيد المحددة، ومخالفة شروط التصاريح، وتقديم معلومات غير صحيحة، ومخالفة ضوابط الأسعار والإعلانات والعروض الترويجية.

مخالفات البناء: تشمل إقامة مبانٍ دون ترخيص، وإجراء تعديلات جوهرية دون موافقة مسبقة، وعدم تجديد تصاريح البناء، والحفر دون تصريح، ومخالفة اشتراطات البناء والسلامة في مواقع الإنشاءات.

الغرامات والتدابير الإدارية:

تتفاوت الغرامات المالية بحسب نوع المخالفة، وفقًا للجداول الواردة في اللائحة، وتُحدد الغرامات بالدرهم الإماراتي. وفي حال تكرار المخالفة للمرة الثانية، تُضاعف الغرامة، بينما تُضاعف الغرامات للمخالفات اللاحقة بشرط ألا يتجاوز إجمالي الغرامات مبلغ 100,000 درهم.

كما يجوز للمركز، بالإضافة إلى الغرامة، اتخاذ تدبير واحد أو أكثر من التدابير التالية:

إغلاق المنشأة لمدة لا تتجاوز ستة أشهر.

مصادرة أو حجز البضائع المضبوطة أثناء ارتكاب المخالفة.

إلغاء الترخيص.

ويتعين على المنشآت المرخصة الالتزام بمتطلبات اللائحة وشروط التراخيص والتصاريح المعتمدة، تفاديًا للغرامات والتدابير الإدارية المقررة.

2. Ultimate Beneficial Ownership (UBO)

Under UAE Cabinet Decision No. 109 of 2023 concerning the Regulation of Beneficial Owner Procedures, all companies established in the United Arab Emirates, including entities registered in commercial free zones, are required to maintain accurate, complete and up-to-date records of their Beneficial Owners, partners and shareholders.

Licensed entities must submit the required information to the relevant Licensing and Registration Authority upon request and within the prescribed timelines.


       Objectives of the Decision

The Decision aims to:

Strengthen transparency within the UAE business environment

Establish minimum obligations for registrars and legal entities in relation to licensing and registration procedures

Regulate the establishment and maintenance of the Register of Beneficial Owners and the Register of Partners or Shareholders

Develop effective and sustainable mechanisms for the collection, management and protection of beneficial ownership data



Definition of a Beneficial Owner


A Beneficial Owner is a natural person who ultimately owns or controls a legal entity, whether directly or indirectly through a chain of ownership or by any other means of control.

This includes:

A natural person who owns or controls 25% or more of the shares or voting rights of the legal entity.

A natural person who exercises ultimate effective control over the entity; or

A natural person on whose behalf transactions are conducted,

in accordance with Article (5) of Cabinet Decision No. (109) of 2023.


Statutory Registers

Companies shall prepare, maintain and keep at their registered office:

Register of Partners or Shareholders (pursuant to Article 10)

Register of Beneficial Owners (pursuant to Article 8)

Such registers must be accurate, complete, and available to the Authority upon request.


UBO Identification Requirements


1. Natural Person (Individual)

To identify and verify a natural person as a UBO, the following documents must be submitted:

Valid passport copy (clear and legible)

Emirates ID (if UAE resident)

Valid residence visa (if applicable)

Signed UBO Declaration confirming ownership or control status

The Authority may request additional documentation where necessary for verification purposes.


2. Legal Person (Corporate Shareholder)

Where a shareholder is a legal person, the entity must provide:

Valid trade license and/or certificate of incorporation

Memorandum and Articles of Association (or equivalent constitutional documents)

Register of shareholders or partners indicating ownership percentages

Corporate structure chart clearly tracing ownership to the natural person(s)

Board resolution authorizing ownership or shareholding (where applicable)

Supporting documentation evidencing indirect ownership or control

Entities are required to trace ownership through all intermediate entities until the natural person(s) exercise ultimate ownership or effective control are identified.


Ongoing Compliance Obligations

Licensed entities are required to:

Maintain an updated Register of Ultimate Beneficial Owners

Notify the Authority of any changes to UBO information within the legally prescribed timeframe

Retain UBO records in accordance with applicable legislation

Ensure that all submitted information remains accurate, complete and up to date



    Cabinet Decision No. 109/2023 – Regulation of Real Beneficiary Procedures

Cabinet Resolution No. (109) of 2023 establishes requirements for identifying and recording the Real Beneficial Owners of legal entities in the UAE, promoting transparency and supporting efforts to combat money laundering and terrorist financing.

Legal entities must identify any natural person who directly or indirectly owns or controls 25% or more of the capital or voting rights. Where no such person can be identified, the applicable control criteria under the Resolution must be followed.

Key Compliance Requirements

Maintain statutory registers: Establish and maintain a Real Beneficiary Register and a Partners or Shareholders Register containing accurate, complete and up-to-date information.

Identify beneficial owners: Record the identity, nationality, address, identification details and basis of beneficial ownership for each relevant individual.

Meet prescribed deadlines: Establish the required registers within 60 days of the applicable date and update changes to beneficial ownership information within 15 days of being informed.

Submit information: Provide the required registers to the Registrar within 60 days of implementation of the Resolution or licensing and registration, as applicable. Additional information requested by the Registrar must be provided within 14 days.

Report changes: Notify the Registrar of amendments to relevant information within 15 days of the change.

Retain records: Preserve the required information and registers in accordance with the Resolution, including applicable record-retention requirements during dissolution or liquidation.

Protect information: Handle beneficial ownership information in accordance with the Resolution's confidentiality provisions and applicable disclosure requirements.


Penalties and Appeals

Non-compliance may result in administrative penalties imposed by the Registrar. Eligible people may appeal such penalties within 30 days of notification, in accordance with the applicable procedures.

The Resolution repeals Cabinet Resolution No. (58) of 2020 and enters into force on the day following its publication in the Official Gazette.


Cabinet Resolution No. 132/2023 – Administrative Penalties for Violations of Cabinet Decision No. 109/2023

Cabinet Resolution No. (132) of 2023 establishes administrative penalties for violations of Cabinet Resolution No. (109) of 2023 concerning the Regulation of Beneficial Owner Procedures. It applies to legal persons licensed or registered in the United Arab Emirates, including entities operating in non-financial free zones.


Key Violations and Administrative Penalties

The attached schedule specifies penalties for the following violations, with fines and corrective requirements varying according to the type and frequency of the violation:

Beneficial ownership registration: Failure to properly register beneficial ownership details or include the required information in the Beneficial Owners Register. First violation: written warning and correction within 15 days. Second violation: AED 20,000 and a corrective warning. Third violation: AED 40,000 and notification to correct the violation.

Establishing and maintaining the Beneficial Owners Register: Failure to establish the register or maintain its data. First violation: written warning and correction within 30 days. Second violation: AED 50,000 and a corrective warning. Third violation: AED 100,000 and notification to correct the violation.

Updating the Beneficial Owners Register: Failure to update the register. First violation: written warning and correction within 15 days. Second violation: AED 15,000 and a corrective warning. Third violation: AED 30,000 and notification to correct the violation.

Nominee Board Director information: Failure to provide the Registrar with the required information concerning a nominee Board Director or Director. First violation: written warning and correction within 30 days. Second violation: AED 40,000 and a corrective warning. Third violation: AED 80,000 and notification to correct the violation.


Register of Partners or Shareholders: Failure to establish the register. The schedule specifies AED 50,000 for the second violation and AED 100,000 for the third violation. Failure to update the register within 15 days of becoming aware of changes attracts a written warning for the first violation, AED 15,000 for the second, and AED 30,000 for the third. Failure to maintain the required partner or shareholder details attracts a written warning for the first violation, AED 30,000 for the second, and AED 60,000 for the third.

Submitting and safeguarding statutory registers: Failure to provide the Beneficial Owners Register and the Register of Partners or Shareholders to the Registrar, or to protect them against damage, loss or destruction. First violation: written warning and correction within 30 days. Second violation: AED 15,000 and a corrective warning. Third violation: AED 30,000 and notification to correct the violation.

Additional information requests: Failure to provide additional data requested by the Registrar within 14 days. First violation: written warning and correction within 30 days. Second violation: AED 15,000 and a corrective warning. Third violation: AED 30,000 and notification to correct the violation.

Complex ownership structures: Failure to disclose the ownership layers of the Beneficial Owner. First violation: written warning and correction within 30 days. Second violation: AED 50,000 and a corrective warning. Third violation: AED 100,000 and notification to correct the violation.

Authorised UAE-resident representative: Failure to provide the Registrar with the name of a natural person residing in the UAE who is authorised to disclose the required information. First violation: written warning and correction within 30 days. Second violation: AED 10,000 and a corrective warning. Third violation: AED 20,000 and notification to correct the violation.

Disclosure of share issuances: Failure to disclose to the Registrar the issuance of shares in the names of persons or directors within 15 days of issuance. First violation: written warning and correction within 30 days. Second violation: AED 15,000 and a corrective warning. Third violation: AED 30,000 and notification to correct the violation.

Transfer of registers during liquidation: Failure to deliver the Beneficial Owners Register and the Register of Partners or Shareholders to the liquidator within 30 days of the liquidator's appointment. First violation: written warning and correction within 30 days. Second violation: AED 5,000 and a corrective warning. Third violation: AED 10,000 and notification to correct the violation.

Record retention by liquidators: Failure to retain the required registers and records for five years from dissolution, liquidation or write-off. The prescribed penalty is AED 100,000.


Additional Enforcement Measures

In addition to the prescribed fine and without prejudice to penalties or procedures under other applicable legislation, the Registrar may suspend the commercial licence and close the commercial premises of a legal person committing a third-time violation. The suspension may be lifted once the prescribed fine has been paid and the violation has been corrected by fulfilling the applicable requirements.


Implementation and Final Provisions

The Cabinet may amend, add to or remove the fines specified in the Resolution.

Administrative fines are collected through the means determined by the Ministry of Finance. For calculating fines, part of a day is considered a full day, and part of a month is considered a full month.

The Minister, in coordination with the Registrar, may issue the directives and procedures necessary to implement the Resolution.

Cabinet Decision No. (53) of 2021 concerning administrative penalties for violations of the previous beneficial ownership procedures is repealed, together with any conflicting provisions.

The Resolution was issued on 15 December 2023, published in Official Gazette Issue No. 766, page 33, and entered into force on the day following its publication.

3. Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

This section contains the anti-money laundering regulatory documents listed by ANCFZ, including the 2025 cabinet resolution referenced on the page.


Regulations on Anti-Money Laundering


Federal Decree by Law No. (10) of 2025 – Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation Financing

Federal Decree by Law No. (10) of 2025 establishes the UAE's legal framework for preventing and combating money laundering, the financing of terrorism, and proliferation financing. It applies to relevant financial institutions, Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions (DNFBPs), Virtual Asset Service Providers (VASPs), non-profit organisations, and other persons and entities subject to its provisions.


Key Regulatory Requirements

Risk Assessment and Internal Controls: Financial institutions, DNFBPs and VASPs must identify, assess, document, manage and continuously update money laundering and terrorism financing risks. They must establish and regularly review internal policies, controls and procedures approved by senior management.

Customer Due Diligence (CDD): Regulated entities must identify and verify customers and Beneficial Owners, understand the nature and purpose of business relationships, establish ownership and control structures, and conduct ongoing transaction monitoring based on assessed risks.

Suspicious Transaction Reporting: Suspected transactions or funds potentially connected to criminal proceeds, money laundering, terrorism financing, or proliferation financing must be reported directly and without delay to the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU), with supporting information and any additional information requested.

Record-Keeping: Relevant entities must retain transaction records, documents and data and make them immediately available to competent authorities upon request, in accordance with the applicable Executive Regulations.

Prohibition of Anonymous Accounts: Opening or maintaining accounts, conducting transactions, or providing services under anonymous, fictitious, alias or numbered names is prohibited.

Targeted Financial Sanctions: Entities must promptly implement instructions issued by the Executive Office or other competent authorities concerning targeted financial sanctions, including applicable freezing measures and restrictions.

Licensing and Registration: Financial activities, DNFBP activities and VASP activities must not be conducted without the required license, registration, or enrolment from the competent authority.

Beneficial Ownership Transparency: The Decree provides for obligations relating to Beneficial Owners, companies, nominee directors and shareholders, legal arrangements, and trustees. The identification procedures for Beneficial Owners are to be regulated by a Cabinet resolution.

Confidentiality: Information relating to suspicious transactions and offences under the Decree is confidential and may only be disclosed where necessary for investigations or proceedings, or as otherwise permitted by law.


Reporting, Supervision and Enforcement

The Decree establishes an independent Financial Intelligence Unit within the Central Bank to receive, analyse and disseminate suspicious transaction reports and related information. Supervisory authorities conduct risk assessments, inspections and compliance monitoring and may impose administrative measures for violations, including warnings, fines, restrictions on activities, restrictions on responsible personnel, suspension of activities and licence revocation.

The FIU may order the cessation or temporary suspension of suspicious transactions for up to 10 working days and may order the freezing of suspected funds for up to 30 days, subject to extension by the Attorney General or their delegate. The Public Prosecution and competent courts may order the tracing, seizure or freezing of funds and criminal property, impose restrictions on their disposal, and order confiscation where legally justified.



Criminal Penalties

The Decree prescribes substantial penalties for violations, including:

Money laundering: Imprisonment for one to ten years and a fine of AED 100,000 to AED 5,000,000, or an amount equivalent to the value of the relevant criminal property, whichever is greater. Enhanced penalties apply in specified circumstances, including recidivism and commission through organised criminal groups or non-profit organisations.

Financing of terrorism: Life imprisonment or temporary imprisonment for at least ten years, together with a fine of AED 1,000,000 to AED 10,000,000, or twice the value of the relevant criminal property, whichever is greater.

Proliferation financing: Temporary imprisonment and a fine of AED 1,000,000 to AED 10,000,000, or twice the value of the relevant criminal property, whichever is greater.

Legal persons: Entities whose representatives, directors or agents commit money laundering, terrorism financing or proliferation financing on their behalf may face fines of AED 5,000,000 to AED 100,000,000, or an amount equivalent to the value of the criminal property, whichever is greater. Courts must order dissolution and closure following conviction for terrorism financing or proliferation financing.

Other violations: The Decree provides additional penalties for failures involving suspicious transaction reporting, confidentiality, beneficial ownership information, targeted financial sanctions, unlicensed activities and other specified obligations. Depending on the offence, penalties may include imprisonment, fines, confiscation and deportation of foreign offenders in the circumstances specified by law.

International Cooperation and Asset Recovery

The Decree provides for cooperation with foreign authorities, mutual legal assistance, exchange of information, execution of qualifying foreign judicial orders, and the identification, tracing, freezing, seizure and confiscation of criminal property. It also establishes mechanisms for national coordination, statistical reporting and the protection of confidential information.

Repeal and Entry into Force

Federal Decree by Law No. (20) of 2018 on Anti-Money Laundering, the Financing of Terrorism and the Financing of Illegal Organisations is repealed. Existing Executive Regulations, resolutions and circulars issued under the previous legislation remain effective to the extent that they do not conflict with the new Decree, until superseded. The Decree was issued on 30 September 2025 and enters into force two weeks after its publication in the Official Gazette.

Cabinet Resolution No. 134 of 2025


Executive Regulations on Anti-Money Laundering, Combating the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation Financing

Overview

Cabinet Resolution No. (134) of 2025 establishes the Executive Regulations implementing Federal Decree by Law No. (10) of 2025 on Anti-Money Laundering (AML), Combating the Financing of Terrorism (CFT), and Proliferation Financing (CPF). It sets out detailed obligations for Financial Institutions, Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions (DNFBPs), Virtual Asset Service Providers (VASPs), Non-Profit Organizations, companies, registrars, and relevant supervisory and competent authorities.


1. Scope and Definitions

The Resolution defines the entities, activities and persons subject to AML/CFT/CPF requirements, including Financial Institutions, DNFBPs, Commercial Gaming Operators, VASPs, Beneficial Owners, Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs), Senior Management, and high-risk customers.

Financial Institutions include businesses engaged in deposits, lending, financial leasing, money transfers, payment services, securities trading, investment management, insurance-related investment activities, and currency exchange.


DNFBPs include:

Commercial Gaming Operators conducting qualifying financial transactions of AED 11,000 or more.

Real estate brokers and agents involved in property purchases or sales.

Dealers in precious metals and stones conducting qualifying cash transactions of AED 55,000 or more.

Lawyers, notaries, independent legal professionals, and independent accountants when performing specified financial or commercial transactions for customers.

Company and Trust Service Providers performing specified incorporation, management, trustee or nominee services.


VASPs include persons commercially conducting activities such as exchanging virtual assets for fiat currencies or other virtual assets, transferring virtual assets, safeguarding virtual assets or related control instruments, and providing specified services connected with virtual asset offerings.


2. Risk Assessment and Risk Mitigation

Financial Institutions, DNFBPs and VASPs must identify, assess, understand, document, manage and regularly update their money laundering, terrorism financing and proliferation financing risks.

Risk assessments must consider customer profiles, geographic exposure, products, services, transactions and delivery channels. Entities must consider the National Risk Assessment and relevant sectoral assessments, retain supporting documentation and provide it to competent authorities upon request.

Internal policies, controls and procedures must be approved by Senior Management, proportionate to the nature and size of the business, monitored for effectiveness and updated continuously.


Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) must be applied where risks are elevated. Measures may include collecting additional customer and Beneficial Owner information, verifying the source of funds and wealth, investigating transaction purposes, increasing monitoring frequency and obtaining Senior Management approval before establishing or continuing a business relationship.

Simplified Due Diligence may be applied to appropriately identified low-risk situations, subject to the Resolution's conditions and coordination with the Supervisory Authority. It must not undermine applicable Targeted Financial Sanctions requirements or be used where a crime is suspected.

High proliferation financing risks require proportionate enhanced controls, documented mitigation measures and periodic reviews designed to prevent violations of, or circumvention of, Targeted Financial Sanctions.


3. Customer Due Diligence and Beneficial Ownership

Customer Due Diligence (CDD) must generally be conducted before or during the establishment of a business relationship, when opening an account, or before carrying out an occasional transaction. Identity verification may be deferred in qualifying low-risk cases only under the conditions specified in the Resolution.


CDD must be applied when:

A business relationship begins.

A crime is suspected.

Previously collected identification information is doubtful or inadequate.

A Financial Institution conducts occasional transactions of AED 55,000 or more, including apparently linked transactions.

A Financial Institution conducts occasional wire transfers of AED 3,500 or more.

A VASP conducts occasional transactions of AED 3,500 or more, including apparently linked transactions.


Entities must identify customers and verify their identities using reliable, independent sources. For individuals, this includes relevant identity, nationality, address, birth and identification-document information. For legal persons and legal arrangements, it includes legal form, registration and constitutional documents, registered address, tax information where applicable, and relevant Senior Management details.


Entities must understand the customer's business, ownership and control structure, and the purpose and intended nature of the relationship. They must verify the authority and identity of persons acting on behalf of customers and conduct ongoing transaction monitoring to ensure activity is consistent with the customer's profile and assessed risks.


Beneficial Owner identification must follow a risk-based approach. For legal persons, the natural person who ultimately owns, individually or jointly, an actual controlling ownership interest or shares of 25% or more must be identified. If ownership does not establish the Beneficial Owner, or doubts remain, the person exercising legal or actual control must be identified. If no such person can be identified, the relevant natural person or persons holding Senior Management positions must be identified.

For trusts and other legal arrangements, identification must cover the relevant Trustee, Settlor, Trust Protector, Beneficiaries or beneficiary classes, and other persons exercising ultimate effective control, as applicable.



Existing customers and relationships must be reviewed according to materiality and risk to ensure previously collected CDD information remains adequate.

Where CDD cannot be completed, entities must not establish or continue the business relationship or execute the transaction and must consider submitting a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) where appropriate. Where applying CDD could alert a customer to a suspected crime, the Resolution permits the specified exception, subject to reporting the reasons for the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU).

Entities must not deal with Shell Banks or open or maintain anonymous accounts or accounts under obviously fictitious names.


4. Politically Exposed Persons and High-Risk Countries

PEPs include persons entrusted with prominent public functions domestically or abroad, senior government officials, senior executives of state-owned enterprises, senior political party officials and prominent officials of international organizations. The definition also covers specified immediate family members and close associates.

For foreign PEPs, entities must establish appropriate identification and risk-management systems, obtain Senior Management approval before establishing or continuing a relationship, take reasonable measures to establish the source of funds and wealth, and conduct enhanced ongoing monitoring.


For domestic PEPs and persons entrusted with prominent functions in international organizations, the specified additional measures apply where the business relationship presents high risk.

Financial Institutions must also assess the risks associated with beneficiaries of life insurance and investment-related insurance policies and apply enhanced measures where the relevant beneficiary presents higher risk.

Business relationships and transactions involving persons from designated high-risk countries or jurisdictions with deficiencies in AML/CFT/CPF systems require proportionate Enhanced Due Diligence and any applicable countermeasures directed by the Supervisory Authority or National Committee.


5. Suspicious Transaction Reporting and Confidentiality

Financial Institutions, DNFBPs and VASPs must establish and continuously update indicators for identifying suspicious activity.

Where a transaction, attempted transaction or funds, regardless of value, are suspected or reasonably suspected to be connected with criminal proceeds, a crime, or intended criminal use, the entity must notify the FIU immediately and without delay. Reports must contain available information about the transaction, funds and related parties, and entities must respond promptly to requests for additional information.


Applicable reporting obligations cannot be avoided by invoking banking secrecy, professional secrecy or contractual liability, subject to the specific legal-professional exceptions provided in the Resolution.

Entities, their directors, officers and employees must not disclose to customers or other persons that an STR has been or will be submitted, that related information has been provided, or that an investigation is underway, except where the Resolution permits information sharing.


6. Internal Controls and Compliance Officers

Entities must maintain documented internal AML/CFT/CPF policies and procedures approved by Senior Management. These must include:

Customer Due Diligence and risk-management procedures.

Suspicious transaction identification and reporting procedures.

Compliance management arrangements and an appropriately appointed Compliance Officer.

Employee screening and suitability procedures.

Regular training programmes and workshops.

Independent audit arrangements to test the effectiveness of internal controls.


The Compliance Officer must operate at management level, have appropriate competence and experience, and possess independence in decision-making.

Responsibilities include monitoring transactions, reviewing records and suspicious activity, assessing whether reports should be submitted to the FIU, maintaining confidentiality, reviewing and improving internal compliance systems, preparing periodic reports for Senior Management, arranging employee training, and cooperating with competent authorities.




7. Record-Keeping and Financial Transaction Controls

Relevant entities must retain transaction records, business documents, CDD information, account files, correspondence, identification documents, STRs, analytical results and applicable recordings for at least five years.

The applicable retention period depends on the record type and relevant event, including transaction completion, termination of a business relationship, account closure, completion of an occasional transaction, inspection, investigation or final court judgment. Records must be organized so individual transactions can be reconstructed and traced, and must be made available promptly to competent authorities upon request.


Correspondent banking: Financial Institutions must assess respondent institutions, understand their business and supervisory arrangements, evaluate their AML/CFT/CPF controls, obtain Senior Management approval before establishing new correspondent relationships, and clarify each institution's responsibilities. Relationships with Shell Banks and institutions permitting Shell Banks to use their accounts are prohibited.


Money or Value Transfer Services: Providers must be licensed or registered with the relevant Supervisory Authority, maintain updated agent lists, include agents in compliance programmes and monitor their compliance.


Wire transfers: International transfers of AED 3,500 or more must include accurate originator and beneficiary information, including names, account numbers or traceable transaction references, and specified originator identification details. Transfers below this threshold must also carry the prescribed information, although verification requirements differ. Domestic transfers must meet the applicable information requirements. Financial Institutions must not execute transfers that fail to comply with the Resolution.

Intermediary and beneficiary institutions must identify transfers with missing information and maintain risk-based procedures for executing, suspending or rejecting them. Applicable Targeted Financial Sanctions measures must also be implemented.


8. Financial Groups, Non-Profit Organizations and Virtual Assets

Financial Groups must implement group-wide AML/CFT/CPF programmes across relevant branches and subsidiaries. These programmes must support appropriate information sharing for CDD, risk management, compliance and audit purposes while safeguarding confidentiality and preventing unauthorized disclosure.

Foreign branches and majority-owned subsidiaries must apply measures consistent with the Resolution to the extent permitted by local law. Where foreign legal requirements prevent implementation, additional risk-mitigation measures and notification to the relevant Supervisory Authority may be required.


Non-Profit Organizations must coordinate with their Supervisory Authority, apply focused and proportionate risk-based measures, adopt best practices to prevent misuse for terrorism financing, maintain clear policies promoting accountability and transparency, and use regulated financial channels where possible. These obligations also apply to persons acting on their behalf or for their benefit.

VASPs must obtain the required licence, registration or listing. They must collect, retain and securely transmit accurate originator and beneficiary information for virtual asset transfers, provide relevant information to competent authorities upon request, and comply with applicable Targeted Financial Sanctions. Relevant requirements also apply to Financial Institutions conducting virtual asset transfers for customers.


9. Transparency and Beneficial Ownership Information

The Resolution establishes obligations for company registrars, companies, nominee directors, nominee shareholders, trustees and other relevant persons.

Registrars must maintain company information and Beneficial Owner records that are adequate, accurate and up to date, verify relevant information and make it available to competent authorities. Companies must comply with applicable information and registration requirements, while nominee arrangements must be appropriately identified and documented.


For trusts and other legal arrangements, the applicable parties must maintain and provide information identifying the relevant persons and their roles, including persons exercising ultimate effective control.

Banking secrecy, professional secrecy and contractual liability cannot be invoked to obstruct the disclosure of information where the Resolution requires it, subject to the applicable legal provisions and exceptions.







10. Financial Intelligence Unit and National Committee

The FIU operates as the national centre for receiving, analysing and disseminating STRs and other relevant information to Law Enforcement Authorities. It has operational independence in carrying out its functions and may request information, conduct analysis, exchange information with counterpart units and disseminate relevant findings.


The National Committee supports national coordination and risk-based AML/CFT/CPF measures. Its functions include relevant coordination, policy and risk-assessment responsibilities under the applicable framework.


11. Supervisory Authorities and Enforcement Powers

Supervisory Authorities oversee compliance by Financial Institutions, DNFBPs, VASPs and Non-Profit Organizations within their respective mandates.

Their responsibilities include assessing risks, issuing instructions and regulatory requirements, reviewing compliance systems, obtaining information, conducting supervision and inspections, and taking appropriate measures against non-compliance. Licensing and registration processes must also address suitability and prevent criminals or their associates from exercising prohibited ownership or control.

The Resolution establishes additional oversight requirements for VASPs, including measures to identify and address unlicensed virtual asset activities.


12. Provisional Measures and Investigations

The FIU may order the suspension or cessation of a suspected transaction for up to ten working days, without prior notice, based on its analysis of STRs or other relevant information.

The FIU may also order the freezing of funds suspected of being linked to a crime for 30 days without prior notice. The Attorney General or delegate must be notified and may amend or revoke the decision or issue appropriate directions. The freezing period may be extended by the Attorney General or delegate upon a justified request from the FIU.

The Resolution also establishes procedures for investigations and the exercise of competent authorities' powers to obtain relevant information, identify and trace criminal property, and support the implementation of applicable legal measures.


13. National and International Cooperation and Asset Recovery

The Resolution provides for coordination and information sharing among national authorities and cooperation with foreign counterparts in accordance with applicable UAE legislation, treaties and agreements, or the principle of reciprocity.

International cooperation covers information exchange, mutual legal assistance, investigations, identification and tracing of criminal property, freezing and seizure of assets, confiscation, recovery of proceeds and execution of qualifying foreign judicial orders.

Authorities must process cooperation requests effectively and promptly, coordinate asset recovery efforts, and observe applicable procedures and protections for third-party rights.


14. Repeal and Entry into Force

Cabinet Resolution No. (10) of 2019 concerning the previous Executive Regulations is repealed, together with any provisions that conflict with Cabinet Resolution No. (134) of 2025.

The Resolution was issued on 29 October 2025 and provides that it enters into force 30 days after publication in the Official Gazette. The actual calendar effective date depends on the date of publication.

Corporate Tax Guide for Free Zone Persons


4. Corporate Tax Guide for Free Zone Persons

Overview

The UAE Corporate Tax framework is governed by Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022 on the Taxation of Corporations and Businesses, as amended by Federal Decree-Law Nos. 60 of 2023, 40 of 2024 and 28 of 2025. The legislation establishes corporate tax rates, eligibility for exemptions, rules for Free Zone Persons, taxable income calculations, deductions, transfer pricing, registration, filing and payment obligations.

1. Corporate Tax Rates

Corporate Tax is imposed on the taxable income of persons covered by the legislation.

0% applies to the portion of taxable income that does not exceed the prescribed threshold for the general corporate tax regime.

9% applies to taxable income exceeding that threshold.

For a Qualifying Free Zone Person (QFZP), 0% applies to Qualifying Income, while 9% applies to taxable income that is not Qualifying Income.

A separate top-up tax framework may apply to qualifying multinational enterprises, subject to the applicable rules.


2. Qualifying Free Zone Person

A Free Zone Person is a juridical person incorporated, established or registered in a UAE Free Zone, including a branch of a Non-Resident Person registered in a Free Zone.

To qualify for the special corporate tax treatment, a Free Zone Person must satisfy all the conditions specified in Article 18 of the legislation, including:

Maintaining adequate substance in the UAE.

Deriving Qualifying Income as specified under the applicable Cabinet decisions.

Not having elected to be subject to the standard corporate tax regime under Article 19.

Complying with the arm's length principle for related-party transactions under Article 34.

Complying with the applicable transfer pricing documentation requirements under Article 55.

Meeting any additional conditions prescribed by the Minister.

A Free Zone licence alone does not automatically qualify a business for the 0% rate. Eligibility depends on meeting the applicable conditions and maintaining compliance.


3. Qualifying Income and Non-Qualifying Income

A QFZP is subject to the following treatment:

Qualifying Income is taxed at 0%.

Taxable income that does not qualify as Qualifying Income is taxed at 9%.

The legislation leaves the detailed definition and treatment of Qualifying Income to the applicable Cabinet decisions and related rules. Businesses must therefore assess their income streams and transactions against the relevant provisions rather than assuming that all Free Zone revenue is eligible for the 0% rate.

A QFZP that fails to meet the prescribed qualifying conditions may lose its qualifying status from the beginning of the relevant Tax Period, subject to any exceptions or alternative timing rules prescribed by the Minister.


4. Election to Apply the Standard Corporate Tax Regime

Under Article 19, a Qualifying Free Zone Person may elect to be subject to the standard corporate tax rates under Article 3(1). The election takes effect from the beginning of either the Tax Period in which the election is made or the following Tax Period, as applicable.

Businesses should evaluate the consequences of such an election before making a decision.


5. Taxable Persons and Scope of Taxation

The legislation distinguishes between Resident Persons and Non-Resident Persons.

Resident Persons may include UAE-incorporated juridical persons, foreign juridical persons effectively managed and controlled in the UAE, and natural persons conducting a business or business activity in the UAE.

Non-Resident Persons may be subject to Corporate Tax on income attributable to a UAE Permanent Establishment, qualifying State-Sourced Income, or a relevant UAE nexus specified under the legislation.

Resident juridical persons are generally subject to Corporate Tax on taxable income derived from within and outside the UAE, subject to applicable exemptions and other provisions.



6. Taxable Income and Accounting Requirements

Taxable income is determined separately for each Taxable Person using adequate standalone financial statements prepared under accounting standards accepted in the UAE.

Accounting income may require adjustments for:

Exempt income.

Allowable deductions and reliefs.

Unrealised gains or losses where applicable.

Related party and Connected Person transactions.

Tax losses and permitted loss relief.

Other adjustments or incentives prescribed under the legislation.

Businesses must maintain appropriate accounting records to support the calculation of taxable income and the information reported in their tax returns.


7. Exempt Income, Deductions and Tax Reliefs

The legislation provides exemptions for specified categories of income, including qualifying dividends and distributions, income from qualifying Participating Interests, qualifying foreign Permanent Establishments, and certain income from international transportation, subject to the relevant conditions.

Eligible business expenditure may be deductible when calculating taxable income. However, certain expenses are expressly non-deductible, including specified fines and penalties, bribes or illicit payments, dividends and profit distributions to owners, Corporate Tax itself, and other items listed in the legislation.

The legislation also provides for qualifying reliefs, including Small Business Relief where the applicable revenue threshold and other prescribed conditions are met, as well as relief for qualifying tax losses and certain business restructurings.


8. Related-Party Transactions and Transfer Pricing

Transactions and arrangements between Related Parties must comply with the arm's length principle. Their terms and outcomes should be consistent with those that independent parties would agree under comparable circumstances.

The legislation recognises transfer pricing methods including:

Comparable Uncontrolled Price Method.

Resale Price Method.

Cost-Plus Method.

Transactional Net Margin Method.

Transactional Profit Split Method.

An alternative method may be used where the prescribed conditions are satisfied.

Where the applicable thresholds and conditions are met, a Taxable Person must maintain a master file and local file. The Federal Tax Authority may also request disclosures and supporting documentation. Where such documentation is requested under Article 55, the statutory period is generally 30 days unless a later date is directed by the Authority.


9. Tax Registration and Deregistration

Taxable Persons must register for Corporate Tax with the Federal Tax Authority (FTA), obtain a Tax Registration Number and comply with the applicable registration timelines, subject to prescribed exceptions.

Where a business ceases its activities, it must apply for tax deregistration in the prescribed form and within the applicable timeline. Deregistration is not generally completed until outstanding Corporate Tax and administrative penalties have been paid and all required tax returns have been filed.





10. Tax Returns and Payment Deadlines

A Taxable Person must file its Corporate Tax Return no later than nine months after the end of the relevant Tax Period, unless the FTA directs otherwise.

Corporate Tax payable must also be settled within nine months after the end of the relevant Tax Period, or by another date determined by the Authority.

Tax returns must contain the required details, including the Tax Period, taxpayer identification information, taxable income, applicable loss relief and tax credits, and Corporate Tax payable.


11. Financial Statements and Record-Keeping

The FTA may request financial statements used to determine taxable income. The Minister may also require specified categories of Taxable Persons to prepare and maintain audited or certified financial statements.

Taxable Persons must generally retain records and documents supporting their tax returns and enabling taxable income to be determined for seven years following the end of the relevant Tax Period. Exempt Persons must also retain records supporting their exempt status for the prescribed seven-year period.


12. Tax Credits, Foreign Tax and Refunds

Corporate Tax payable is settled using available withholding tax credits, foreign tax credits and any other applicable credits or incentives in the prescribed order.

Foreign Tax Credit is generally limited to the Corporate Tax payable on the relevant income. Unused Foreign Tax Credit cannot be carried forward or backward under the legislation.

A Taxable Person may apply for a refund where withholding tax credits exceed Corporate Tax payable or where the FTA determines that excess Corporate Tax has been paid, subject to the applicable procedures.


13. Anti-Abuse Rules

The legislation contains a general anti-abuse rule intended to prevent arrangements designed to obtain a Corporate Tax advantage that is inconsistent with the law's purpose.

The FTA may examine relevant transactions and arrangements and make appropriate determinations under the statutory conditions. Businesses should ensure that their transactions reflect genuine commercial purposes and that their records support the tax treatment adopted.


Conclusion

The UAE Corporate Tax framework provides a special tax treatment for qualifying Free Zone businesses, including a 0% rate on Qualifying Income. However, the benefit is conditional. Free Zone Persons must maintain adequate substance, meet the applicable income and other eligibility conditions, comply with transfer pricing rules, maintain appropriate records, register where required, and submit tax returns and payments within the prescribed deadlines.
